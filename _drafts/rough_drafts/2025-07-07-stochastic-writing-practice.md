In Twyla Tharp's _The Creative Habit_, she describes an exercise in creativity that I have always been obsessed with. "Create Order from Chaos" is about as simple as it gets: drop a collection of coins on a table and rearrange them into a more pleasing shape. The activity is an exercise in creating order from chaos such that creativity will become easier to activate when necessary. Creativity is not something that happens when inspiration strikes. It's something that happens intentionally—a muscle you can build. Lately I've been working a lot through off-the-wall writing activities, and I've been thinking how we might practice writing using the same low stakes, stochastic tactics as the "Create Order from Chaos" exercise. Here are a few different ways for doing so.

Each of these approaches is meant to be exercised in less than five minutes as a way to stretch your creative muscle. In each case, I think it's important that you try not to write multiple *related* sentences. Doing so would start to lean too much in the direction of giving them a shape, of raising the stakes. Lean into the fragmentary nature of the individual sentence. 


1. Use a random word generator to find some trigger word or phrase. Write three different sentences. The sentences can be inspired by the word or incorporate it directly.

2. Go to a window and look outside. What do you see? Pick one thing and write three sentences about it, inspired by it, or incorporating it. 

3. Pick up a random book close at hand, open it, and point to a random word on a random page. Write three sentences, incorporating that word or inspired by it. 

When we write, we're usually trying to say something specific. By practicing free-form writing in the ways above, we'll be ready to go when we sit down to say something important. As John Coltrane would say, "We practice so when the doors of perception open, we're prepared to step through."
