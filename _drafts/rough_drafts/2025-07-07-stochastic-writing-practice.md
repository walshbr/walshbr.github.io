This one's called stochastic writing practice

in Twyla farp book, The creative habit,

she has described this exercise that I talk about, think about all the time, where she'll drop a collection of coins on a table, see what random pattern they fall into, and see what patterns she can draw out of it, and then perhaps rearrange them into a more pleasing shape. It's meant to be an exercise in creativity, one in which you practice creating order from chaos, and also just practice finding whatever patterns there might be in the chaos around you, such that when you sit down To actually be creative in a way that you need to be you just have an easier time of doing it. You're practiced at it. Creativity. Creativity is not something that happens when inspiration strikes. It's something that happens intentionally. It's a muscle you can build. It's a habit you can live by, and if you practice it'll be easier to activate that habit when you need to do it. So I was just one. I was thinking about how we might practice using the same technique. It's kind of low stakes, stochastic practice for working and strengthening a creative muscle as it as it pertains to writing. Here are a few different ways for doing so. First, use a random word generator to find some trigger word or phrase. Here are a couple examples, if you wanted to get specific to a particular set of vocabulary for a particular kind of discipline, you might pull up a particular article or book related to your work, flip to a random sentence, point to a random sentence With your finger, and then write three different sentences about it. They can either be inspired by this, the word that you point to, they can incorporate the word that you point to. But the three sentences should not be connected in any way. I think that's important, because if you start to connect them, I think you might start to raise the stakes for what you're trying to say. In this case, you're really just trying to come up with some sentences that you can really just throw away. The idea is to develop some sentences in a low stake manner. Second, go to your window. Look outside. What do you see? Pick one thing you see, write two, three sentences about it, inspired by it, incorporating it. Make sure the sentences aren't connected to each

other, and then third,

pick up a random book, close at hand, point to a random sentence on a random page. Write three sentences, incorporating that word or inspired by it. You might also just try to rewrite the sentence that you're pointing at three different ways, and that's it. You come up with an infinite number of related exercises, but the idea is that they're meant to be low stakes, easily practiced, sort of thing that you can do in five minutes every day to practice a creative approach to writing in a way where you're not actually trying to say anything in particular. The important piece is just that you're practicing using your words and using the creative muscle so that when you actually need to do so, it's easier to do. I.
