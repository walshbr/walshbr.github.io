This might feel like a massive tangent, and maybe it will feel that way after you read it in the read this post in its entirety, but I had this wild idea that I wanted to try. What would a writing program that asks you to write every day look like if it was modeled on a kind of couch to 5k or other kinds of running programs? I've been obsessed with this idea, and I've tried it out in the month of April, and I've developed a plan for myself, and I'm going to see how it goes. So my real goal, right, is to spend some time working on a book project, something that is often getting kicked to the back burner as I have other kinds of writing and other kinds of things I need to work on. So it's a common, common writing or not so common running, cliche that you never want to add distance or frequency, I think, or distance or speed, try to double check that. So my goal here is not to write a lot. It's just to make sure that I write every day in some capacity. So without further ado, here is the wild plan that I've made for myself. I'm going to treat it as an eight week program and follow on the couch to 5k and I'm going to start out the first week every day writing one sentence, just one one sentence that feels crafted to the extent that I don't need to come back and fuck with it, but it is a complete thing that I will add pebble by pebble to my final product over the course of the month. So the first week, I do one sentence a day, every day, and then each i. Week I add another sentence. So week two gets two sentences a day. Week three gets three sentences a day, etc, etc, until by week eight, I'm doing eight sentences a day. This might feel ridiculous. What is the point of writing one sentence? That's essentially a paragraph per day that would be wildly productive and would really add up quickly.
