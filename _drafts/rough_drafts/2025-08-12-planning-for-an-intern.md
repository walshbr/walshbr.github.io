This semester, I've got Winnie Perez, Martinez, former practice fellow, working with me in the lab as an intern through our PhD Plus Program at the University of Virginia. These internships are meant to be experienced based jobs of about 10 hours a week that replaces students teaching in the hopes of introducing them to the kinds of skills and experiences that they'll need to pursue a variety of different kinds of careers in academia or outside of it. I've never had someone report directly to me doing work related to the work that I'm doing outside of like a research context. So I've been trying to give a lot of thought to what it might mean to be a responsible supervisor, and also what it might mean for this internship to really be constructed in a way that provides Winnie with a positive and fulfilling experience. I've talked with her several times about her plan, and this is my take on what we are going to be trying to do together, such that she gets the maximum

impact from what she's doing with us first.

From a structural standpoint, we're going to be setting up during the course of the semester, weekly 30 minute scrums at the beginning of each week, in which we'll talk about what we're going to be doing for this week. We're going to be answering three questions, what did I do? What's next for me, and what do I need from somebody else? And we'll also be talking about plans, and at the end of the week, that 30 minutes will be mirrored 30 minute meeting on Friday afternoons where we debrief everything that went on that week. That'll hopefully offer some frame for what we're going to be doing together. And by my own admission, I tend to work mostly independently with self established deadlines and accountability really lies with yourself. So typically, when I work with students, I'm pretty accepting and pretty flexible, but Winnie asked for a bit more structure and accountability. Is this is what we came up with in terms of what Winnie is going to be working on. We're aiming for a mix of different kinds of things. When I first sat down to talk about this process with her, I said, you know, there are going to be things that are specifically only useful for me in my job. There are going to be things that are really interesting for you, but not especially useful to the day to day workings on that are going on. And then there are going to be things that are somewhere in between, where they're in fulfilling and invigorating for you, but also add to the work that I'm doing, take stuff off my plate, pursue new research projects and that sort of thing. So in general, I told Winnie I was very uninterested in having her work on things that were solely of use to me and not fulfilling it all for her, and really wanted to lean on the other two categories, perhaps with a sweet spot being those things that were fulfilling for both of us. And so we white boarded some things out here are all the different kinds of tasks that we came up with that she might work on with us. Ultimately, we decided on a mix of different kinds of things, some of which I'll talk about in a later blog post, but I wanted to give some broad buckets for the kind of work that win eight will be doing. She's going to be sitting in on some meetings with us as appropriate. So I don't get a ton of just sort of random one off consultations, which is to say, you know, some of the other people in the lab get more requests for one off discussions of digital projects. Mine tend to be more student focused, pursuing new and Introduction to Digital humanities or asking questions about the practice fellowship. So we're going to have her sit in on a couple of those meetings as appropriate. She's going to be blogging, of course, as a way to try and fill out her professional profile. She is sitting in on planning meetings for our fellowships, and even going to be running a couple idea sessions for us. The idea behind these is that these are somewhere in between consultations and independent work. For example, I had Winnie sit in on the meeting where I plan out the semester for praxis, giving her a look at curriculum design and what it looks like to take what we've done each year, build on it, map it out. So I introduced her to my process for how I set things up for the year, everything from, okay, I sit down, I copy over the stuff from last year's calendar, I update the dates, I invite people, I think about what needs to be changed, I communicate with the students, all that good stuff. And then there are the actual projects that I'm going to be working on for her or with her. There are four that I have in mind, and she'll talk a little bit more about those in future blog posts. Those are I want her to update our documentation for our fellowship committees. So that a couple years ago, when I was on paternity leave, I put together a pretty extensive, like, 10 page document for Laura Miller, when she was kind of, when she was chairing one of our fellowship committees in my absence that told her everything that needed to happen from like, this is the date where the where the CFP goes out. And like, do I communicate with the students DGS first, or the graduate school first, or do I let them know about a fellowship first? Those sorts of things.
