[SLIDE ON BILL MURRAY]

In the 1993 Bill Murray comedy, Groundhog Day, a weatherman gets trapped in a time loop, endlessly repeating the same day over and over again. It's often billed as a family friendly comedy, but I think it's actually quite dark. It's about the sort of the existential crisis that you feel when faced to relive the everyday over and over again, where your actions don't really matter, and it's a meditation on the kinds of small actions, small changes that you can make in a single day, whether they matter and ultimately, what your place is in the world. In a way, it's about the creeping horror of the everyday when there is a larger, world-ending crisis going on that everyone is ignoring.

And frankly, the experience of Bill Murray in this movie of reliving the same thing over and over again is not at all dissimilar from my experience writing the piece that we're discussing today.
