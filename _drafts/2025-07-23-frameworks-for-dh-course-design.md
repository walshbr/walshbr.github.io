I've been thinking a bit about DH course design and the ways we construct our courses at a high level have pedagogical implications before we put in any content. There are, of course, any numbers of ways you might put together a class. But I have noticed some common patterns in how I've seen people organize things, and they could be helpful for folks designing a DH course for the first time. Here are some common ways of structuring things along with some links to example course sites where I can find them. 

* Topical organization - This category comprises those courses that are divided by particular topics of interests. A course might start with a week on "What is DH?" before moving to other weeks on topics like "Postcolonial DH" or "DH Critiques." In many ways this sort of course is probably the most typical and probably mirrors the same kind of construction in other disciplines. EXAMPLES NEEDED 
* Discipline plus DH – Examples of this genre might be "Text mining the Novel" or "Digital Approaches to History." That is, courses framed like this take digital methods and apply them to otherwise typical disciplinary materials. I often think of these courses as requiring special care lest they inadvertently try to cram multiple classes into a single semester. In other words, a "Hacking the Book" course might wind up being at once a semester-long intro to programming, a literature course, and also a combination of the three. 
* Organized by method – Readings and project examples will be slotted in accordingly, but the guiding signposts for the course are techniques rather than topics. For example, a semester might be broken into three units on texts, archives, and maps. Probably better suited to a course 
* Hack vs Yack — In courses that meet twice a week, one neat structural choice an instructor can make is to designate one meeting as discussion and the other as a regular lab day. So Monday of a particular week might deal with spatial questions and then Wednesday would introduce ArcGIS. The option makes it easy to divide things up, but it can also be easy for the structure to enforce artificial distinctions between critique and method that you might not want.
* Dataset as through line - In this framework, students are assigned a particular dataset that is flexible enough to be applied to a variety of different methodologies.
