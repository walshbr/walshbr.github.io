Lately I've been playing some retro games from my childhood. I started with the Breath of Fire series. Revisiting these games has been a fascinating view into those things that change with distance. Those sprawling dungeons that seemed to take hours as a child turn out to be just a few rooms as an adult. An unstructured plot that leads you to wander an over world map turns out to be bread crumbed pretty significantly if you talk to the right NPC.[^1] The dialogue that I found captivating as a child can feel ham-fisted with the benefit of years.[^2] 

Playing these games one after the other has been an interesting view into how much a game studio can develop in just one year. Breath of Fire II has more complex dungeons, moving dialogue, and a plot that hits you from moment one. The first Breath of Fire largely structures itself around small story beats as you chase an evil organization around the world. In true JRPG fashion, you eventually fight God for Reasons™. The opening of Breath of Fire II still takes my breath away. In it, you join your father and sister on a visit to a long-sleeping dragon who mysteriously appeared to defend your town the same night that your mother disappeared. As a player familiar with the series that features characters able to transform into dragons, it's easy to connect the dots and gather that you are communing with your sleeping mother. The game invites you to hang back alone and close your eyes to hear the dragon's voice. After doing so, you wake up to find that what remains of your family has disappeared and all of the NPCs you talked with mere moments ago act as though they have never met you.
